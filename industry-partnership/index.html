Technical evaluation for industry and applied R&D

Does BAITSSS add a capability your existing system does not already have?

BAITSSS is a published, process-based modeling system for field-scale evapotranspiration, soil-water dynamics and irrigation analysis. It is not presented as a replacement for sensing hardware, irrigation control, machinery integration, remote-sensing products or a complete agronomy platform. The relevant question is narrower: whether its simulation and state-tracking capability adds measurable value inside an existing research, product or water-analysis workflow.

Evidence before positioning. BAITSSS has peer-reviewed field evaluations and applications, but performance is not assumed to transfer unchanged to every crop, climate or implementation. Desktop software verification and scientific field validation are also different questions. See Scientific Stewardship and the publication record.
Where it can fit

A complementary scientific layer, not another full platform.

The strongest use case is where an organization already has observations, products or control systems and wants an independent process-based trajectory that can be inspected, rerun and compared under documented assumptions.

Benchmarking

Independent model comparison

Compare proprietary ET, soil-water or irrigation outputs with a separate two-source water-and-energy-balance implementation using a predefined evaluation dataset.

Scenario analysis

Rerun documented assumptions

Change supported inputs or management assumptions and examine how ET, soil water and irrigation trajectories respond through time.

Historical analysis

Reconstruct periods without a sensor network

Use satellite vegetation information and meteorological forcing to investigate historical periods, subject to input availability and model limitations.

Spatial analysis

Field variability at Landsat scale

Represent within-field spatial variability at 30 m rather than treating a single installed point sensor as the entire field.

Research

Inspectable scientific state

Hourly state progression, separated soil and canopy processes, provenance and exportable results support model intercomparison and applied research.

Integration

Test incremental value first

Integration is worth discussing only if a controlled evaluation shows useful information or functionality that is not already available in the existing system.

Published evidence

What has actually been demonstrated?

The record is mixed in the way real field-model evaluation usually is: strong results in some settings, identifiable error in others, and continuing sensitivity to inputs and assumptions. The numbers below are presented so a technical evaluator can judge the evidence directly rather than relying on adjectives such as “mature.”

7% cumulative ET error

Blind, uncalibrated Bushland test

In a 127-day 2016 corn evaluation against a weighing lysimeter in semiarid, advective conditions, the initial blind BAITSSS simulation produced 7% cumulative ET error, daily RMSE of 1.70 mm d−1, and daily r² of 0.66. Documented model revisions reduced the cumulative discrepancy to less than 1%, daily RMSE to 1.09 mm d−1, and daily r² to 0.86.

Dhungel et al., 2019, Hydrological Processes
r² ≈ 0.77 · RMSE ≈ 0.94 mm d−1

California citrus comparison

A 2024 study compared BAITSSS with eddy-covariance ET and the OpenET ensemble at two citrus orchards. BAITSSS showed mixed results relative to observations, with approximately r² = 0.77 and RMSE = 0.94 mm d−1. The study explicitly emphasizes the difficulty of ET estimation in evergreen, clumped citrus canopies.

Dhungel et al., 2024, Agricultural Water Management
≈110 km² · 78 days

Regional Yuma/Bard application

BAITSSS was applied across a major irrigated agricultural area spanning Yuma Valley, Arizona and the adjacent Bard region of California. The 2025 study showed that soil-property choice materially changed simulated ET and irrigation, directly documenting an important source of model uncertainty rather than hiding it.

Dhungel et al., 2025, Agricultural Water Management
Multiple crops and climates

Published lineage, not universal validation

Published BAITSSS work includes Idaho and northern California development, Texas lysimeter and eddy-covariance evaluations, California citrus, and regional irrigation modeling in Arizona/California. That establishes a research history and range of applications. It does not establish that every crop, location, parameterization or software pathway has been field validated.

Comparable precedent

Research-origin ET methods can become operational tools.

The useful precedent is not that ag-tech companies acquire other companies. It is that scientific ET methods developed in research settings can become operational components of real water-management systems after extensive evaluation. That is a closer analogy to the pathway BAITSSS would have to earn.

METRIC moved from university research into routine agency use

The Idaho Department of Water Resources states that METRIC was originally developed in collaboration with the University of Idaho and is routinely used for basin water-budget studies and groundwater models.

Idaho Department of Water Resources

eeMETRIC became an operational consumptive-use method

After a nine-year Upper Colorado River Basin study, the Upper Colorado River Commission adopted eeMETRIC for agricultural consumptive-use measurement, and the Bureau of Reclamation transitioned to eeMETRIC for related reporting. OpenET provides the operational framework.

OpenET / Upper Colorado River Commission pathway
What this precedent does and does not show: it demonstrates that research-origin water-use methods can move into operational workflows when their performance, transparency and practical value are established. It does not demonstrate that BAITSSS will be licensed, integrated, acquired or adopted by any particular organization.
Positioning discipline

State the proposition narrowly enough to test it.

Weak message

“BAITSSS is another ET and irrigation model for growers.” That asks an established platform to care about a category it may already cover and gives no measurable reason to evaluate the system.

Defensible message

“BAITSSS is a published process-based field water and energy-balance system. We would like to test whether it provides useful independent ET, soil-water, irrigation-trajectory, scenario-analysis or benchmarking capability on one dataset where your organization already has a trusted comparison.”

Evaluation route

Define the pass/fail question before running the model.

A technical evaluation should not end with “the curves look similar.” The comparison target, metrics and decision rule should be agreed in advance and should match the proposed use case.

0115-minute technical screen

Confirm that the organization has a real use case that BAITSSS could plausibly address.

02Select one representative dataset

Use a field with a trusted reference such as lysimeter or eddy-covariance ET, measured water inputs, soil-water observations, or an established internal benchmark.

03Predefine metrics

Choose metrics appropriate to the question: cumulative ET bias, RMSE/MAE, correlation, soil-water trajectory, irrigation timing, spatial behavior, or an explicitly defined product capability.

04Run transparently

Record inputs, assumptions, parameter changes and model version. Do not rescue a poor comparison with undocumented tuning.

05Decide

Continue only if the agreed metrics are acceptable or the evaluation reveals a useful capability that the existing workflow genuinely lacks.

There is no universal BAITSSS pass threshold.

A validation study, an internal R&D benchmark and a product-integration test answer different questions. The organization and BAITSSS team should define the acceptance criterion before the comparison. Failure against that criterion is a valid outcome and should end or redirect the discussion.

Who evaluates fit

Start with people who can judge the science and product gap.

Relevant functions include applied R&D, agronomy, irrigation science, remote sensing, product science, technical product management and innovation. Partnerships or corporate development become relevant only after technical fit is established.

Digital agronomy and irrigation platforms

Evaluate whether BAITSSS adds an independent process model, scenario capability or historical reconstruction that the existing stack does not provide.

Remote-sensing and water-intelligence teams

Evaluate whether a process-based trajectory adds useful interpretation, benchmarking or state information alongside observational products.

Applied research and institutional water teams

Evaluate whether BAITSSS provides a reproducible field-scale analysis environment suitable for the research or water-management question at hand.

Credibility anchor

Scientific lineage and current software work should be distinguishable.

BAITSSS originated in University of Idaho research and has since been developed and evaluated through peer-reviewed work involving Kansas State University, USDA Agricultural Research Service and University of California collaborators. The current desktop software effort packages that scientific system into a usable Windows application. Those facts provide scientific lineage and engineering context; they are not presented as institutional endorsement or as proof that the current desktop implementation is accurate in every setting. Review the publications and scientific stewardship framework.

Evaluate one real case before discussing a transaction.

Choose a representative field or dataset, agree on the comparison and acceptance criteria, document the run, and determine whether BAITSSS adds scientific or product value. Only a positive technical result should lead to a discussion of collaboration, access, licensing or integration.
