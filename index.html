Published application · Western Kansas

Applied irrigation fell sharply. Did crop water demand fall with it?

BAITSSS provided an independent estimate of expected crop water use and irrigation requirement from vegetation, weather, soil, and water-balance conditions. That reference helped distinguish a change in irrigation management from a change in environmental demand.

Scientific workflow

Prepare. Verify. Simulate. Examine. Evaluate.

BAITSSS connects the major stages of field-scale modeling so the scientific path from input data to final results remains visible and reviewable.

Prepare and verify

Define the project, spatial domain, period, environmental inputs, and model readiness before computation begins.

Simulate hourly

Carry the coupled surface energy balance and soil water state forward through the complete simulation period.

Examine and evaluate

Inspect mapped results, pixel time series, exports, and comparisons with independent observations when available.

Scientific foundation

A process-based model carried through time.

Energy balance

Soil and canopy exchange are represented separately within the surface energy balance.

Water balance

Surface and root zone stores carry water conditions between hourly steps.

Traceable workflow

Inputs, preparation, settings, assumptions, warnings, run identity, and outputs remain reviewable.

Peer reviewed research record

Developed through published field-scale evapotranspiration research.

The BAITSSS publication record spans 2016 through 2025, including studies of semiarid agriculture, restricted water allocation, advective environments, lettuce, citrus, and soil heterogeneity. The desktop software builds on that research history, while software verification and current scientific evaluation remain separate.
