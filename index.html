Satellite and geospatial analysis

↗

Find relatively warm and cool areas

August 27, 2013, Kansas: warm and cool areas across the region, with the target area marked.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The map displays Landsat-derived land surface temperature (LST), not air temperature. The color scale represents temperature and the marked target locates the field within the regional scene.

How to use it. Compare warm and cool surfaces across the region before interpreting conditions at the target. Differences may reflect vegetation, soil exposure, moisture, weather and management; the map alone cannot distinguish these causes.

↗

Explore vegetation patterns

NDVI for the same acquisition shows how vegetation cover varies across the landscape.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The map shows normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) for the same Landsat acquisition. Higher NDVI generally indicates stronger green vegetation signals; lower values can indicate sparse vegetation or exposed soil.

How to use it. Use the vegetation map together with the temperature map to distinguish spatial patterns associated with canopy cover from thermal contrasts that warrant further investigation.

↗

Put your field in regional context

The star locates the target-area mean within the regional NDVI–temperature population.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The density plot combines paired NDVI and LST values for regional pixels. Color intensity indicates where observations concentrate, while the marked target mean locates the project within that relationship.

How to use it. This lets users compare a field with a relevant regional population rather than relying on absolute temperature alone. Similar NDVI does not guarantee identical crop type, soil or water status.

↗

Investigate repeated thermal contrasts

Cool and warm seasonal patterns across Kansas pivots are relative to same-date pixels of similar NDVI, not direct water-stress estimates.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The seasonal map summarizes relative LST differences (ΔLST) calculated against reference pixels of similar NDVI within each acquisition. Negative and positive values indicate cooler and warmer conditions relative to that reference.

How to use it. Repeated relative contrasts can identify locations for targeted investigation. The product is not a direct measure of ET, irrigation or water stress, and its interpretation depends on the available valid comparisons.

↗

Screen vegetation for irrigation modeling

Seasonal NDVI percentiles and threshold trials show how many agricultural pixels would be retained or excluded from modeled irrigation eligibility, not actual irrigation.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The guidance panel summarizes seasonal NDVI statistics and evaluates candidate vegetation thresholds. A threshold trial changes which agricultural pixels would qualify for modeled irrigation under the screening rule.

How to use it. Users can assess whether a proposed threshold excludes sparsely vegetated areas or retains too much of the domain before committing a simulation setting. It is a model-input decision, not satellite detection of applied irrigation.

↗

Evaluate modeled temperatures against observations

June 24, 2013: canopy temperature versus Landsat LST, with spatial differences, paired-pixel density and error distribution for 30,544 paired pixels.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The diagnostics combine a modeled-minus-observed temperature map, paired-pixel density and error distribution for a matched acquisition. Bias describes systematic difference; RMSE summarizes the magnitude of disagreement.

How to use it. The spatial map locates discrepancies while the distributions show their prevalence. Landsat LST and modeled canopy temperature are different physical quantities, so disagreement must be interpreted in light of the selected temperature component.

Published applications

Science applied to real water problems.

Texas · Corn · 2019

Independent field evaluation

In a 127-day Bushland corn study, the blind test reported 7% cumulative ET error against weighing-lysimeter measurements. This is study-specific, not a universal accuracy guarantee.

Groundwater allocation analysis

Compare seasonal water use

Published 2021–2022 orchard study compared BAITSSS with eddy covariance and OpenET. Two-year mean annual ET: 867 mm modeled and 908 mm measured by EC, using recorded irrigation. Site-year performance varied.
