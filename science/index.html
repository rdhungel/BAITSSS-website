Science

A coupled energy and water balance.

BAITSSS scientific model supported by peer-reviewed research. View study evidence.

BAITSSS is both a scientific model and a scientific software platform. Transparent operation does not remove the need to evaluate assumptions, data, and uncertainty.

Model architecture

Two-source energy balance

Soil and canopy exchange are represented separately within the surface-energy balance.

Flux partitioning

Soil evaporation and canopy transpiration contribute to total evapotranspiration.

Two-layer soil water

Surface and root-zone stores carry water conditions between hourly steps.

Vegetation

Vegetation indices inform changing canopy properties and model parameterization.

Meteorological forcing

Hourly weather provides the environmental forcing for energy and water exchange.

Irrigation logic

Configured vegetation and root-zone thresholds determine modeled replenishment.

Resistance framework

BAITSSS uses coupled aerodynamic, canopy, and soil resistance formulations within the two-source energy-balance solution. These formulations influence modeled temperatures and the partitioning of evaporation and transpiration.

Resistance connectivity, parameter sensitivity, stability behavior, and transferability remain active areas for systematic scientific evaluation.

Model state and feedback

Weather supplies forcing while vegetation affects radiation and exchange. Energy balance determines fluxes and modeled temperatures; evaporation and transpiration deplete soil-water stores. Precipitation and configured irrigation replenish water, and the resulting state affects subsequent time steps.

Vegetation and temperature: three distinct roles

Vegetation observations

NDVI and LAI are prepared from Landsat imagery within the developing workflow.

Model temperature state

BAITSSS calculates soil and canopy temperature states internally as part of the two-source energy-balance solution.

Thermal observations

Satellite/UAV thermal observations are an area of active research and are not currently part of the verified Phase I assimilation pathway.

Crop-water configuration

Irrigation requirement is a modeled condition, not a reconstructed farm record.

BAITSSS couples hourly soil-water accounting with vegetation development. Irrigation is triggered when the configured root-zone condition reaches its threshold, subject to crop-development rules.

Default interpretation

Relatively unstressed crop condition

The standard configuration estimates the irrigation needed to avoid substantial modeled crop-water stress.

Intentional alternative

Deficit-irrigation scenario

A user may deliberately configure a different crop-water target. That scenario must be labeled intentionally.

Critical distinction

Required ≠ applied

Without observed irrigation-event data, BAITSSS calculates modeled irrigation requirement. It does not claim the exact amount actually applied by a producer.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Input coverage, spatial alignment, soil properties, initial moisture, and vegetation parameterization affect results.
  • Greenness may not represent physiology during stress, senescence, or in some perennial systems.
  • Modeled irrigation requirement is not a measurement of farmer-applied water.
  • Each new crop, climate, and management setting requires independent validation.
  • Software execution success does not establish scientific accuracy.

How BAITSSS complements current tools

BAITSSS is not presented as a replacement for ArcGIS Pro, OpenET, or other satellite products. It adds a coupled hourly energy and soil-water accounting workflow that carries crop and root-zone conditions through time.
