BAITSSS Desktop capabilities

Hourly evapotranspiration, soil-water balance and irrigation simulation using weather-station or spatial environmental inputs.

Desktop V1 is in pre-release development and testing. Thermal optimization is an optional research feature.

Overview

BAITSSS Desktop integrates project management, input preparation, model execution and results analysis in a Windows application.

Technical capabilities

1. Projects and study areasProject management, geographic boundaries and installation

Projects and simulations

  • Create, open and manage multiple independent projects
  • Save simulation periods, input datasets and model settings
  • Retain and reopen previous runs and results

Area of interest (AOI)

  • Draw or import geographic boundaries, including supported shapefiles
  • Inspect boundaries against maps and rasters
  • Check coordinate reference system, extent and grid alignment
  • Retain imported feature names in supported AOI and Results views

Windows installation

  • Packaged application with scientific and geospatial runtime
  • Shared environmental datasets separated from project data
  • User projects and results stored separately from application files
2. Satellite and environmental dataLandsat, NLDAS, soil, elevation and land cover

Landsat

  • Landsat 7, 8 and 9 scene discovery and preparation
  • NDVI and LAI on the 30 m model grid
  • Vegetation interpolation between valid observations
  • Scene dates, quality information and source records

Environmental layers

  • Elevation and DEM
  • NLCD land cover
  • Soil field capacity and available water capacity
  • Automatic selection, review and replacement of supported layers

Hourly meteorology

  • NLDAS hourly forcing
  • Reuse local data and acquire missing inputs
  • Check hourly coverage, spatial alignment and valid-data extent
  • Reference ET preparation
3. Upper Route — weather-station simulationHourly simulations using local meteorological and vegetation data

Station inputs

  • Import hourly weather CSV files
  • Station latitude, longitude and elevation
  • Air-temperature and wind measurement heights
  • Check timestamps, meteorological variables and period coverage
  • Optional vegetation and irrigation inputs

Weather and reference ET

  • Original and conditioned weather series
  • Weather conditioning settings and comparison plots
  • Short and tall reference ET calculations and comparisons
  • Selection of weather series for BAITSSS simulation

Simulation and results

  • Hourly energy and soil-water balance using station inputs
  • User-supplied or simulated irrigation
  • Soil properties, initial moisture, layer depths, canopy resistance, albedo and emissivity settings
  • Hourly graphs, CSV outputs and simulation reports
The station-based simulation workflow is implemented in the development build. Matched BAITSSS ET comparisons between original and conditioned weather remain under testing.
4. Lower Route — spatial simulationLandsat-based field and landscape analysis

Spatial inputs

  • 30 m Landsat-based model grid
  • AOI-aligned vegetation, weather and static layers
  • Input coverage and valid-cell checks

Simulation

  • Whole-AOI spatial runs
  • Point and single-cell runs where available
  • Parallel processing of spatial blocks within each hour
  • Sequential hourly soil-water state updates
  • Short diagnostic runs and seasonal simulations

Run management

  • Run-specific inputs, settings and outputs
  • Progress monitoring and hourly checkpoints
  • Reopen saved simulations and compare management settings
5. Energy balance, soil water and irrigationHourly model formulation and management settings

Energy and water balance

  • Two-source surface energy balance
  • Total ET, vegetation transpiration and soil evaporation
  • Surface and root-zone soil-water balance
  • Soil and canopy temperatures
  • Hourly transfer of soil-water states

Management and irrigation

  • Modeled irrigation requirement
  • Management allowable depletion (MAD)
  • Rooting depth and surface-layer depth
  • Initial soil moisture and soil hydraulic properties
  • Canopy resistance, albedo and emissivity

Scenario analysis

Compare simulations under different soil, vegetation and management settings. Modeled irrigation represents the requirement under the selected rules; applied irrigation can be evaluated using field records.

See the model science for equations and assumptions.
6. Landsat vegetation and thermal analysisSpatial and temporal analysis of observed surface temperature

Landsat observations

  • Land surface temperature (LST) maps
  • NDVI versus LST scatter plots
  • Selected-pixel NDVI, LST and relative temperature histories
  • Seasonal mean relative LST maps

Temperature comparisons

  • Observed LST with actual satellite acquisition times
  • Comparison with modeled temperature at matched times
  • AOI and selected-pixel temperature analysis
  • Scene source and quality records

Model evaluation

Landsat temperature observations are used for comparison with the hourly model. The combined modeled temperature diagnostic is undergoing evaluation against radiometric LST.

7. Parameter sensitivity and thermal optimizationOptional experimental workflows for Upper and Lower routes

Upper Route experiments

  • User-supplied hourly pixel-scale CSV data with weather, vegetation and thermal observations
  • Independent parameter trials using the hourly model
  • Gaussian-process and random-search comparisons
  • Training and holdout temperature error and ET time series

Lower Route experiments

  • Use a completed spatial Original run and its prepared inputs
  • Prepare matched Landsat thermal observations and temperature comparisons
  • Configure separate parameter studies and candidate runs
  • Checkpoint-capable candidate execution

Parameter evaluation

  • Minimum canopy resistance, rooting depth, MAD and soil/canopy albedo
  • Compare temperature, total ET, transpiration, evaporation, soil moisture and irrigation
  • Retain Original simulations and candidate outputs separately
Optimization is optional and experimental. Independent field ET and water measurements are needed to assess changes in accuracy. Methods and preliminary results →

10. Computational performance and recoverySpatial processing, numerical consistency and long simulations

Execution and recovery

  • Persistent workers and within-hour spatial multiprocessing
  • Reuse prepared weather inputs and raster data
  • Stop, Pause and Resume from completed hourly checkpoints where available
  • Disk-space checks and lossless storage of older hourly outputs
  • Run-specific finalization and hourly CSV output

Numerical consistency

The Float32 hourly soil-water state conversion used in the earlier file-based implementation is retained in memory. Six variables matched the reference calculation in a 3,096-hour single-pixel regression test.

Performance tests

  • Approximately 60-fold speedup in a matched 168-hour single-pixel calculation
  • Kansas spatial test: 615,648 cells over 3,096 hours in 37 min 53 s of grid computation
  • Spatial timing excludes preparation and finalization; tests used a Windows 11 laptop with Intel Core Ultra 9 285H and 32 GB RAM
These measurements describe the manuscript test configurations, rather than total application runtime or performance on other hardware.
