From satellite observations to understanding water use.

Explore six complementary capabilities, from independent Landsat analysis to continuous simulation and model evaluation. Open any image at full size or expand its explanation for scientific context.

01 · Observe

How does our field compare with its surroundings?

What you can do: Explore Landsat vegetation and land-surface temperature from the regional scene down to the field.

↗

Regional surface temperature

August 27, 2013, Kansas: warm and cool areas across the region, with the target area marked.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The map displays Landsat-derived land surface temperature (LST), not air temperature. The color scale represents temperature and the marked target locates the field within the regional scene.

How to use it. Compare warm and cool surfaces across the region before interpreting conditions at the target. Differences may reflect vegetation, soil exposure, moisture, weather and management; the map alone cannot distinguish these causes.

↗

Regional vegetation

NDVI for the same acquisition shows how vegetation cover varies across the landscape.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The map shows normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) for the same Landsat acquisition. Higher NDVI generally indicates stronger green vegetation signals; lower values can indicate sparse vegetation or exposed soil.

How to use it. Use the vegetation map together with the temperature map to distinguish spatial patterns associated with canopy cover from thermal contrasts that warrant further investigation.

↗

Regional temperature distribution

The target-area distribution (yellow) differs from the regional population (blue), providing context for field temperature.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The histogram compares the frequency distribution of observed LST in the target area with that of the larger region. Read temperature along the horizontal axis and relative occurrence on the vertical axis.

How to use it. The position and spread of the target distribution reveal whether it is relatively cool, warm or variable compared with the surrounding landscape. Differences provide context, not a direct estimate of water use.

↗

Field-scale surface temperature

California citrus: local temperature patterns reveal locations worth closer examination.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The field-scale map resolves spatial variation in satellite-derived surface temperature within the California citrus project. The color scale allows comparison among locations within the mapped domain.

How to use it. Users can locate persistent or unusual thermal patterns for follow-up with vegetation, soil, irrigation records and model outputs. A single warm pixel does not establish crop water stress.

Why it matters: A field temperature or vegetation value is more informative in its regional context.

02 · Explore

How do vegetation and temperature interact?

What you can do: Compare paired pixels, examine statistical relationships and follow changes across acquisitions.

↗

Vegetation–temperature density

The star locates the target-area mean within the regional NDVI–temperature population.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The density plot combines paired NDVI and LST values for regional pixels. Color intensity indicates where observations concentrate, while the marked target mean locates the project within that relationship.

How to use it. This lets users compare a field with a relevant regional population rather than relying on absolute temperature alone. Similar NDVI does not guarantee identical crop type, soil or water status.

↗

Interactive NDVI–LST relationship

July 10, 2013: paired pixels show a negative NDVI–temperature relationship (r = −0.815), not direct evidence of irrigation.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. Each plotted point represents a paired vegetation and surface-temperature observation from the selected Landsat scene. The regression summarizes the direction and strength of the relationship.

How to use it. A negative relationship is consistent with cooler surfaces at higher vegetation cover in this scene, but does not identify the cause. Users can explore individual pixels and compare the relationship with other acquisition dates.

↗

Across-scene comparison

Five Kansas acquisitions show that correlations and slopes change through the season.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The table summarizes scene-specific vegetation–temperature relationships rather than pooling all acquisitions into one regression. Compare the reported slopes and correlations across dates.

How to use it. Seasonal changes in canopy development, weather and surface conditions can alter the relationship. This helps determine whether a pattern is repeated or specific to one satellite overpass.

↗

Seasonal relative thermal mapping

Cool and warm seasonal patterns across Kansas pivots are relative to same-date pixels of similar NDVI, not direct water-stress estimates.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The seasonal map summarizes relative LST differences (ΔLST) calculated against reference pixels of similar NDVI within each acquisition. Negative and positive values indicate cooler and warmer conditions relative to that reference.

How to use it. Repeated relative contrasts can identify locations for targeted investigation. The product is not a direct measure of ET, irrigation or water stress, and its interpretation depends on the available valid comparisons.

Why it matters: Similar vegetation can exhibit different temperatures; those contrasts motivate investigation of environmental and management factors.

03 · Understand

Are all agricultural areas behaving alike?

What you can do: Examine seasonal vegetation statistics and thermal–vegetation evidence, then test agricultural screening choices.

↗

Thermal–vegetation evidence

California citrus: descriptive vegetation–temperature classes retain an explicit ambiguous category.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. This result combines vegetation and relative thermal information into descriptive spatial classes. The ambiguous category explicitly retains locations where the observations do not support a clear classification.

How to use it. The map helps organize heterogeneous field conditions and prioritize further inspection. It does not confirm irrigation events or establish whether a particular crop is water stressed.

↗

NDVI irrigation guidance

Seasonal NDVI percentiles and threshold trials show how many agricultural pixels would be retained or excluded from modeled irrigation eligibility, not actual irrigation.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The guidance panel summarizes seasonal NDVI statistics and evaluates candidate vegetation thresholds. A threshold trial changes which agricultural pixels would qualify for modeled irrigation under the screening rule.

How to use it. Users can assess whether a proposed threshold excludes sparsely vegetated areas or retains too much of the domain before committing a simulation setting. It is a model-input decision, not satellite detection of applied irrigation.

Why it matters: Land-cover classification alone does not establish active cropping or irrigation. Observations help identify where closer examination is warranted.

04 · Simulate

What happens between satellite observations?

What you can do: Run continuous hourly water and energy balance simulations, including ET, soil evaporation, canopy transpiration, soil water and modeled irrigation.

↗

Continuous simulation and mapped Results

Inspect simulated spatial results, select locations and examine the continuous water balance in the desktop environment.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The Results interface provides spatial model output and selected-location inspection within a saved project. Hourly simulation carries the energy and soil-water states forward between satellite overpasses.

How to use it. Users can examine ET, its evaporation and transpiration components, soil-water conditions and modeled irrigation through time. These are model predictions conditioned on the inputs and assumptions, not direct satellite measurements.

Why it matters: Water management is continuous, while satellite observations are intermittent. The physical model connects conditions through time.

05 · Evaluate

Can the model explain what the satellite observes?

What you can do: Compare acquisition-matched modeled temperatures and Landsat LST across pixels and through time.

↗

Thermal diagnostics by acquisition

June 24, 2013: canopy temperature versus Landsat LST, with spatial differences, paired-pixel density and error distribution for 30,544 paired pixels.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The diagnostics combine a modeled-minus-observed temperature map, paired-pixel density and error distribution for a matched acquisition. Bias describes systematic difference; RMSE summarizes the magnitude of disagreement.

How to use it. The spatial map locates discrepancies while the distributions show their prevalence. Landsat LST and modeled canopy temperature are different physical quantities, so disagreement must be interpreted in light of the selected temperature component.

↗

Area-of-interest thermal history

Four Kansas acquisitions compare area-mean Landsat LST with modeled soil, canopy and exploratory combined temperatures on matched pixels.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The graph compares area-mean Landsat LST at available acquisitions with the modeled canopy, soil and exploratory combined temperature on matched pixels. The separate series make component behavior visible.

How to use it. Users can identify acquisitions with systematic warm or cool departures and investigate whether discrepancies change across the season. The satellite points constrain only acquisition times, not every intervening hour.

Why it matters: Discrepancies reveal where model assumptions and physical representation warrant investigation.

06 · Investigate improvements

Can thermal observations help improve the model?

What you can do: Compare independent parameter experiments and their impacts on modeled temperature and seasonal ET while preserving the Original run.

↗

Spatial seasonal ET

Original and experimental seasonal ET maps reveal local differences even when domain-average changes are small.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The three panels compare seasonal ET from the Original run, an experimental candidate and their spatial difference. The difference map shows where the parameter experiment changes modeled water use.

How to use it. Spatial inspection is essential because contrasting local changes may cancel in a domain average. A candidate that changes ET is not necessarily more accurate; independent ET observations are required.

↗

Temperature comparison across methods

California citrus: compare modeled temperature series with 13 Landsat acquisitions. Persistent discrepancies remain informative; the combined proxy is unvalidated.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The graph places Landsat acquisition temperatures alongside Original and experimental modeled temperature series. Differences between methods and observations reveal how alternative parameter choices affect thermal behavior.

How to use it. Users can investigate persistent bias and whether improvement at some dates is accompanied by deterioration at others. The combined temperature proxy remains unvalidated and improved thermal agreement does not establish improved ET.

↗

Selected optimization parameters

Compare selected physical parameters and training and withheld-acquisition errors; these are not proof of better ET.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The table records selected parameter candidates and their associated training and withheld-acquisition errors. Parameters represent assumptions about physical processes, not independently measured field properties.

How to use it. Compare performance outside the observations used for fitting and examine whether selected values remain physically plausible. This supports transparent evaluation rather than selecting a method solely because it has the lowest fitting error.

↗

Raster optimization workspace

Lower Route: configure full-period raster experiments while retaining the original run.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The Lower Route workspace exposes the study configuration and parameter bounds for optional spatial raster experiments. It allows candidate runs to be examined separately from the preserved Original simulation.

How to use it. A documented experiment enables users to investigate how parameter choices change spatial and seasonal results without silently replacing the baseline. This is an experimental research capability, not an automatic correction of ET.

↗

Independent CSV optimization workspace

Upper Route: review independent CSV experiments and saved outputs without changing Original simulation.

Explore this result

What you are looking at. The Upper Route workspace supports independent CSV-based parameter experiments using supplied inputs and thermal observations. Its saved results allow the user to inspect trial behavior and compare alternatives.

How to use it. This provides a separate pathway for studying parameter sensitivity and temperature agreement at selected locations. It does not replace the Lower Route spatial workflow or establish that an optimized result is more accurate.

Why it matters: Better thermal agreement is not proof of better ET. Independent ground observations are needed to assess improvements.

Observation analysis does not require simulation. Satellite-derived patterns are not direct irrigation or water-stress measurements. Thermal diagnostics and optimization are exploratory.
