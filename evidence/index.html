Evidence

Evidence should be read according to what each source actually supports.

BAITSSS keeps peer-reviewed evidence, software verification, and current scientific questions visibly separate. Study-specific findings are not generalized beyond their demonstrated scope.

Study evidence

Field and site evaluations

Published BAITSSS studies provide the scientific basis for interpreting model behavior in the locations, periods, observations, and experimental conditions reported in those papers.

Each reported result should be read together with its study design, inputs, comparison data, and limitations.

Interpretation rule

Do not generalize beyond the demonstrated scope

Evidence from one crop, site, climate, period, or comparison does not by itself establish performance for every application. The supporting publication remains the authority for the exact result and its context.

Software verification

Engineering evidence is separate from scientific validation

Execution equivalence

Modernization tests compare new execution paths against accepted scientific reference behavior under defined equivalence criteria.

Checkpoint and restart

Stop, resume, and restart behavior is tested separately so lifecycle reliability can be demonstrated without being confused with scientific accuracy.

Performance

Runtime and execution improvements are engineering evidence. Faster computation does not substitute for independent scientific evaluation.

Ongoing evaluation

Current scientific questions

Resistance behavior and sensitivity

Resistance connectivity, parameter sensitivity, stability behavior, and transferability remain active areas for systematic scientific evaluation.

Independent observations and comparisons

Thermal observations, ET products, field measurements, soil-water observations, and irrigation records provide different forms of evidence and must be interpreted according to their scale, timing, and uncertainty.

Evidence boundary: published evidence, internal software verification, and experimental or ongoing evaluation are intentionally not presented as interchangeable forms of support.
