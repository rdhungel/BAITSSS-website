Market ecosystem research · September 2026

How agricultural water technology actually reaches farms.

The commercial ecosystem around irrigation analytics, evapotranspiration, remote sensing, field sensing and water accounting is broader than a farmer buying a model. Public evidence shows multiple buyer classes, distribution channels and revenue structures. This page records the market evidence and maps organizations whose existing work intersects with field-scale water analysis.

Important: inclusion below does not imply endorsement, partnership, interest in BAITSSS, or a sales relationship. This is a public ecosystem and outreach map built from documented activities. It is intended to identify where real water-management problems, budgets, users and distribution channels already exist.
Core finding

The market does not mainly buy “another model.”

Successful companies tend to sell a decision, an operational workflow, a measurement service, irrigation control, compliance support, enterprise reporting or a quantified economic or water outcome. The scientific model is often the engine underneath the product rather than the object the customer believes they are buying.

1
Farm and grower purchase

Growers pay when the product reduces pumping, labor, crop loss, water use or uncertainty strongly enough to justify the cost.

2
Trusted intermediary

Dealers, irrigation companies, agronomists and consultants bring technology into farms through relationships that already exist.

3
Institutional purchase

Water districts, GSAs, counties, food companies and corporate water programs can pay for capability that reaches many farms at once.

science / algorithm→usable product→specific costly problem→pilot→measured result→reference customer→channel→scale
Commercial proof

Documented examples show how adoption happens.

These examples are useful because they show actual contracts, customer deployments, dealer relationships, integrations or named enterprise programs rather than general claims that agriculture “needs technology.” Vendor case studies are not independent scientific validation, but they are evidence of customer acquisition and commercial structure.

Reinke + CropX: equipment channel

Reinke integrates CropX soil, weather and ET information into ReinCloud and markets the combination through its irrigation dealer network. Reinke has also offered a CropX sensor with new irrigation systems. Source

Lumo + AvidWater: regional implementation partner

Lumo expanded beyond Napa/Sonoma through AvidWater, which sells, installs and supports the smart-valve system. Lumo reported more than 70 vineyards and five of the ten largest U.S. winegrape growers using the system. Source

Hydrosat + Madera County: institutional contract

A three-year renewal covers about 220,000 acres with field-level crop-water monitoring, allocation tracking, irrigation tools and reporting for agencies, landowners and growers. Source

Kilimo + Microsoft: corporate water funding

A California Central Valley project supports growers converting to more efficient irrigation as part of Microsoft’s water-positive strategy, with Netafim among implementation partners. Source

Ceres + growers: economic problem first

Fowler Packing used imagery to identify the true extent of water stress and support an irrigation-system change; Ceres documents the resulting improvement in the affected block. Source

Land IQ + public agencies: professional analysis

Land IQ provides remote-sensing analytics, ET, land-use mapping and custom GIS services to private industry, water districts, regulators and government, including statewide crop mapping with California DWR. Source

Arable: enterprise and research customers

Arable publicly names organizations including Bayer, Netafim, Treasury Wine Estates, University of Nebraska, Embrapa and the World Food Programme, showing a buyer base extending far beyond individual farmers. Source

CropX: platform consolidation

CropX has expanded through acquisitions and enterprise customers including AB InBev, Nestlé, General Mills and McCain Foods, illustrating how technical tools become part of larger platforms and supply-chain programs. Source

What this means for BAITSSS

The missing test is distribution and a costly problem, not another marketing asset.

BAITSSS already has a scientific model, desktop workflow, website, documentation, flyer and video. The unresolved commercial question is whether a specific buyer or intermediary has a problem valuable enough to justify using it. The strongest initial routes are likely to be professional users who can apply one system across many fields: water agencies/GSAs, irrigation or agronomic consultants, research programs, large multi-field operations, or technology companies that need a scientific analysis layer.

Public water route

Test field-level ET, soil-water and irrigation analysis against a real accounting, allocation, monitoring or evaluation problem.

Consultant / dealer route

One technical user can apply BAITSSS across many growers, avoiding the need to teach every farmer a new modeling system.

Research / enterprise route

Use BAITSSS where reproducibility, scenario analysis, traceable inputs and full time-series outputs matter more than a simple irrigation alert.
