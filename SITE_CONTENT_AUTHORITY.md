# BAITSSS Website Content Authority

Prepared: 2026-09-06

## Core rule

Every public page has one primary identity. Full explanations live only on their authority page. Other pages may use the minimum context necessary to orient the reader and then link to the authority page.

Navigation, footer, accessibility text, and unavoidable product naming are structural repetition and are exempt. Scientific claims, explanatory paragraphs, cards, headings, examples, status language, workflow descriptions, and calls to action are not exempt.

## Page authority

- Home: what BAITSSS is and where to go next.
- Software: how the maintained desktop product and project workflow operate.
- Capabilities: what the maintained system can do.
- Science: how the scientific model works.
- Evidence: what has been verified, evaluated, compared, or demonstrated.
- Published Science: visual scientific record and published study results.
- Research & Education: research, teaching, student work, and academic use.
- Access & Participation: how an institution, organization, project, or collaborator can engage with BAITSSS.
- Development & History: scientific and software development history and current development stage.
- Origins & Publications: bibliographic publication authority and scientific lineage.
- FAQ: clarification of genuine ambiguities only. It must not become a second Science, Software, Capabilities, Access, Research, or History page.
- Documentation: navigation to user and technical reference material. It must not restate the content of the pages it indexes.
- Example Project: one concrete end-to-end example, not a second Software or Capabilities page.
- Scientific Stewardship: principles for scientific responsibility, interpretation, and separation of observed, supplied, and modeled quantities.
- Citation: citation instructions only.
- System Requirements: system requirements only.
- Known Issues: current known issues and reporting guidance only.
- Release Notes: release-specific changes only.
- Contact: contact route only.

## Enforcement test

For every public heading, paragraph, card, box, example, and call to action, ask:

1. Does this content belong primarily to this page's identity?
2. Is the same concept already explained more fully on another authority page?
3. If yes, can this be reduced to a short orienting sentence plus a link?
4. Does removing it make the page more focused without hiding essential information?

If yes, consolidate it.

## No duplication by paraphrase

Changing wording does not make duplicated content unique. Two blocks that communicate the same scientific, software, access, evidence, research, or historical message count as redundancy even when the sentences are different.
