Research & Education

Bring the scientific question. Use BAITSSS as the modeling environment.

BAITSSS can support research built around a field experiment, monitoring site, independent dataset, teaching objective, or model-evaluation question.

Research collaboration

Where BAITSSS can contribute

Existing research sites

Apply BAITSSS to an established field site, experiment, or monitoring network with suitable spatial and meteorological inputs.

Independent observations

Evaluate model behavior against eddy covariance, lysimeter, soil water, irrigation, or other appropriate field measurements.

Crop and climate studies

Investigate behavior across crops, climates, soils, management conditions, and meteorological forcing.

Model comparison

Compare BAITSSS with independent ET products, observations, or other modeling approaches while keeping the comparison question explicit.

Methods development

Study resistance architecture, vegetation representation, soil assumptions, thermal information, flux partitioning, and other scientific components.

Funded projects and proposals

Use BAITSSS as the modeling component of a broader project when its scientific scope and development status fit the proposed work.

Scientific workflow

From research question to inspectable results

Research uses the same traceable path as the desktop workflow: prepare the project, verify the inputs, run the hourly simulation, inspect spatial and pixel-level results, and evaluate the model against suitable evidence.

Reproducible setup

Project inputs, simulation period, settings, run identity, and outputs remain associated with the scientific run.

Inspectable output

Mapped results and pixel time series expose spatial and temporal behavior rather than only final totals.

Independent evaluation

Observed and modeled quantities can be compared at appropriate spatial and temporal scales when suitable observations are available.

Education

Follow the physical links in the model

Physical evapotranspiration

Connect radiation, resistance, soil evaporation, transpiration, and atmospheric demand.

Water and energy balance

Follow water storage and surface energy fluxes through time.

Sensitivity experiments

Change controlled assumptions and inspect their effect on later modeled states and fluxes.

Reproducible workflows

Use project inputs, run records, checkpoints, and outputs to reproduce and review scientific work.

Independent evaluation

Compare the model with appropriate evidence

When modeled and observed results differ, the purpose is not to force agreement. The workflow supports checking observations, inputs, assumptions, spatial scale, timing, and model behavior before assigning a cause.

Scientific evaluation remains separate from software verification. A successful run shows that the software executed; it does not by itself establish scientific accuracy.
