Research & Education

Teach the science, not the software plumbing.

BAITSSS gives students a working scientific desktop environment for learning evapotranspiration, soil water, irrigation, satellite data, and field-scale modeling. Time that would otherwise be spent building software infrastructure can instead be used to understand the science, test assumptions, analyze results, and design agricultural water-management studies.

Academic use

BAITSSS does not replace programming education. It provides a ready scientific environment when the educational objective is agricultural water science, model interpretation, and field-scale analysis.

01 · One scientific workflow

From field data to scientific interpretation

A class can move through one connected workflow rather than assembling separate tools for each step.

Landsat + weatherWork with satellite observations and hourly atmospheric forcing.
ET + soil waterExamine coupled energy-balance and soil-water behavior through time.
Irrigation + mapsEvaluate modeled irrigation response and field-scale spatial variability.
CSV + interpretationExport results, test assumptions, compare scenarios, and communicate findings.
02 · Teaching and research

Use across agricultural water programs

COURSES

Classroom and laboratory use

Evapotranspiration, irrigation, hydrology, remote sensing, precision agriculture, crop science, and agricultural engineering courses can use the same desktop workflow.

STUDENT RESEARCH

Undergraduate through PhD

Students can investigate field behavior, model assumptions, sensitivity, spatial variability, irrigation scenarios, and independent comparison without first constructing the full software environment.

FACULTY

Teaching and supervision

Faculty can use one repeatable environment for demonstrations, assignments, theses, capstone projects, model evaluation, and field studies.

03 · Academic package

A low-friction path into the science

The academic package is being organized around materials that let students begin with a working scientific problem rather than a long software setup exercise.

  • BAITSSS Desktop
  • Example project and data
  • Short getting-started tutorial
  • One complete classroom exercise
  • Instructor guidance
  • Explanation of the science behind each workflow
  • CSV and figure outputs for analysis
  • Suggested undergraduate, MS, and PhD project directions
Programming still matters.

BAITSSS is not intended to remove programming from scientific education. It removes unnecessary software-engineering overhead when the objective is to study evapotranspiration, soil water, irrigation, remote sensing, model behavior, and scientific interpretation.

04 · Research directions

Questions students and researchers can investigate

01

Resistance and stability

Evaluate aerodynamic, canopy, soil, and stability assumptions, including conditions that affect hourly numerical behavior.

02

Thermal observations

Compare independent surface-temperature observations with modeled temperature states.

03

Soil and vegetation sensitivity

Test soil properties, rooting assumptions, vegetation representation, and flux partitioning.

04

Crop and site transferability

Examine behavior across crops, climates, management settings, and meteorological conditions.

05

Model comparison

Compare BAITSSS with independent ET products, field observations, and other modeling approaches.

06

Spatial performance

Examine behavior from representative points to gridded fields and heterogeneous landscapes.

05 · Academic participation

Research and teaching through institutions

Academic use is organized through the responsible university, department, laboratory, or research program so the software, study context, and scientific responsibilities are clear.
