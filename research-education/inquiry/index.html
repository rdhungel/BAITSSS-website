BAITSSS
Water-management, research & project inquiries

Start with the problem, the evidence, and the decision that needs support.

Water managers, agencies, consultants, researchers, advisors, and students can begin with the question they are trying to solve. Existing meters, delivery records, weather networks, satellite products, GIS, groundwater models, field observations, or agency data can remain part of the work. The first step is to understand where a field-scale process and scenario analysis can add useful information.

Send four things.

A short first message is enough. These four items help define the technical question without turning the first conversation into a software request.

01 · PLACE

Where?

Field, farm, district, basin, research site, program area, or another study area.

02 · PERIOD

When?

Single event, growing season, year, multi-year study, drought period, or another time window.

03 · QUESTION

What needs an answer?

Conservation, allocation, irrigation, demand, verification, field diagnosis, scenario testing, research, or another technical question.

04 · EVIDENCE

What already exists?

Measurements, irrigation or pumping records, weather, soils, field observations, satellite products, GIS, agency records, or other models already used in the project.

What happens next

Define where BAITSSS adds value within the existing workflow.

BAITSSS can provide hourly field-process analysis, soil-water and irrigation behavior, spatial variability, scenario testing, method comparison, and a reproducible technical record. Depending on the project, that work may support conservation analysis, allocation review, agricultural demand, groundwater or recycled-water studies, drought scenarios, verification, field research, or professional technical analysis. The appropriate pathway can then be project analysis, research collaboration, training, software use, or a broader organizational discussion.
