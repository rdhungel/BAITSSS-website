Scientific Stewardship

From model development to scientific stewardship.

The BAITSSS model was developed to connect satellite observations with hourly weather, vegetation, soil water, and irrigation processes through time. Scientific stewardship extends that responsibility beyond producing a model result. It requires a traceable connection between the data used, the assumptions made, the calculation performed, the uncertainty present, the independent evidence available, and the conclusion ultimately reported.

Stewardship principle

A scientifically useful result is not only a number. It is a result whose origin, configuration, model state, evidence, limitations, and interpretation can be followed and examined.

01 · Stewardship scope

Responsibility continues from data acquisition through interpretation.

As BAITSSS has moved from a research implementation toward BAITSSS Desktop, the scientific task has become broader. Model equations remain central, but the reliability of a result also depends on how data enter the system, how a run is configured and executed, how model states are interpreted, and how uncertainty and independent evidence are handled.

Data

Know what entered the analysis

Satellite observations, meteorological forcing, soils, land cover, terrain, irrigation information, and configured parameters must remain identifiable at the scale and time at which they were used.

Execution

Know what calculation was performed

The BAITSSS model carries water and energy states through time. The exact simulation period, configuration, scientific settings, and execution route therefore belong to the scientific record of the result.

Interpretation

Know what the result can support

Model output must be interpreted within the available observations, assumptions, spatial and temporal scale, scientific evaluation record, and known limitations of the application.

Scientific stewardship is the connection between the scientific model and the scientific claim. BAITSSS Desktop can preserve that connection, but software traceability does not replace scientific judgment or independent validation.
02 · Chain of evidence

Keep observations, supplied information, modeled quantities, and conclusions separate.

A BAITSSS analysis combines information that comes from different sources and has different scientific meaning. Those classes should remain distinguishable throughout analysis and reporting.

Observed

Measurements and observations

Satellite measurements, field observations, meteorological measurements or forcing products, irrigation records, eddy covariance, lysimeter data, and other independent records retain their own measurement basis and uncertainty.

Supplied

Inputs and assumptions

Project geometry, dates, soil properties, rooting assumptions, vegetation information, management settings, initial conditions, and model parameters define the simulation that is being asked of the BAITSSS model.

Modeled

Calculated states and fluxes

Evapotranspiration components, soil-water states, modeled irrigation, resistance behavior, and modeled soil and canopy temperatures are quantities calculated by the BAITSSS model.

Concluded

Scientific interpretation

Statements about performance, management, field condition, agreement, disagreement, or applicability are conclusions. They must be supported by the preceding evidence rather than treated as direct model output.

03 · Uncertainty and sensitivity

A model result carries the influence of its data and assumptions.

BAITSSS model behavior can respond to uncertainty in forcing, vegetation, soil representation, irrigation timing, management assumptions, spatial scale, and the observations used for comparison. Stewardship means identifying these influences rather than reducing uncertainty to one unexplained accuracy number.

Forcing and vegetation

Weather and remotely sensed state

Weather forcing and satellite-derived vegetation information influence energy exchange and water use. Bias, temporal mismatch, spatial scale, and vegetation representation can therefore propagate into modeled fluxes and states.

Soil and initial state

Hydraulic representation matters

Soil hydraulic properties and starting soil-water conditions influence depletion, evaporation, root-zone behavior, irrigation timing, and the amount of water available to the simulated system.

Management

Irrigation assumptions affect the water balance

Known irrigation, modeled irrigation, management allowable depletion, rooting depth, and other management settings are not interchangeable. Their role must be identified when interpreting ET and soil-water behavior.

Scale and comparison

Compare like with like where possible

A 30 m model pixel, a field average, a point measurement, an eddy-covariance footprint, and a regional product do not represent exactly the same spatial support. Timing and aggregation also affect comparison.

04 · Interpreting model states

Model variables describe the simulated system, not automatically the observed field.

Evapotranspiration

ET is a modeled flux

BAITSSS model ET is produced by the coupled water and energy balance. Agreement with an independent ET estimate or measurement is evidence about a specific comparison, not proof of universal accuracy.

Soil water

Soil-water state is conditional on the representation

Surface and root-zone soil-water values reflect the soil properties, initialization, precipitation, irrigation, evaporation, transpiration, and model structure used in that simulation.

Irrigation

Modeled irrigation is not recorded irrigation

When irrigation is simulated, the result represents irrigation required under the configured model rules. It should not be described as farmer-applied water unless independent records establish that correspondence.

Temperature

Modeled temperatures remain model states

Soil and canopy temperatures are calculated internally by the BAITSSS model. Independent thermal observations can be used for scientific comparison, but they do not become the modeled state simply because they are available.

05 · Evidence and validation

Scientific validation and software verification answer different questions.

The BAITSSS model has been evaluated through peer-reviewed studies in multiple agricultural settings. BAITSSS Desktop also undergoes numerical and software verification. These evidence classes strengthen different parts of the system and should not be substituted for one another.

Scientific validation

Does the model represent the field adequately?

Independent measurements and comparison datasets are used to evaluate model behavior for a defined site, period, crop, climate, data source, and experimental context.

Numerical equivalence

Does a changed execution route preserve the science?

Controlled equivalence testing asks whether the current execution system reproduces the accepted scientific reference within defined numerical criteria.

Software verification

Does the desktop system execute and preserve the run correctly?

Project state, data readiness, run identity, Stop and Resume behavior, outputs, provenance, packaging, and other software functions are tested independently from field validation.

06 · Traceability in BAITSSS Desktop

Interpret the exact run that produced the result.

BAITSSS Desktop is intended to keep scientific computation connected to the project record so that a result can be traced back to the conditions under which it was produced.

Run identity

Identify the simulation

Record the project, AOI, simulation period, BAITSSS Desktop version, scientific settings, and run identity associated with the result.

Provenance

Identify the source information

Keep the Landsat, weather, base data, configured values, and other authoritative inputs tied to the run instead of separating the output from its source record.

Reproducibility

Preserve enough context to examine the result again

A scientific result is more useful when another qualified user can understand which data, settings, model version, execution route, and interpretation produced it.

07 · Reporting

A defensible BAITSSS result should carry its context with it.

For research, teaching, collaboration, evaluation, or professional reporting, the scientific record should be sufficient to distinguish the model result from the evidence used to interpret it.

Describe

Data and configuration

State the study area, dates, input sources, soil representation, vegetation information, management assumptions, initial conditions, and material model settings.

Identify

Model and software

Identify the relevant BAITSSS model scientific references and the BAITSSS Desktop build or version used to execute and preserve the analysis.

Separate

Output and evidence

Label modeled quantities, observations, externally derived products, and comparison metrics so that one class is not presented as another.

Evaluate

Independent evidence

When validation data are available, describe their measurement basis, spatial and temporal relationship to the model output, and any aggregation or matching procedure.

Qualify

Uncertainty and limitations

Report known limitations, sensitivity, data constraints, scale differences, and conditions under which the interpretation should not be generalized.

Conclude

Stay within the evidence

Conclusions should follow from the specific analysis and evidence. A successful run, a visually plausible map, or agreement in one case should not be used to claim more than the evidence supports.

08 · Continuing stewardship

Development should expand capability without weakening scientific accountability.

The longer-term BAITSSS direction is a more connected scientific environment in which data acquisition, model execution, field-state interpretation, uncertainty evaluation, broader validation, and future decision-support capabilities can work together. These are development directions, not a statement that every element is already available in BAITSSS Desktop.

Current responsibility

Preserve what is scientifically established

New software architecture, faster execution, additional data routes, and new Results tools should remain traceable to the BAITSSS model science and verified against accepted references where appropriate.

Future responsibility

Evaluate before expanding claims

New forcing sources, forecasting, uncertainty tools, management applications, and decision-support functions require their own scientific testing and clearly stated boundaries before they should be treated as established capability.

The objective is not simply to make BAITSSS produce more information. The objective is to make each result easier to understand, examine, reproduce, compare, challenge, and improve as the scientific system develops.
