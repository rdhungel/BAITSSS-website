Scientific Stewardship

Use the model with its inputs, assumptions, and evidence in view.

BAITSSS outputs are model results. Their interpretation depends on the input data, field conditions, assumptions, model version, and independent observations available for comparison.

Interpreting results

Keep observations, inputs, modeled states, and interpretation separate.

Observed

Satellite measurements, weather observations or forcing products, field measurements, and other independent records represent conditions at their own spatial and temporal scales.

Supplied

Project geometry, dates, soil properties, rooting assumptions, management information, and other configured inputs define the model setup.

Modeled

ET components, water-balance states, modeled irrigation, resistance behavior, and other simulated quantities are calculated from those inputs and assumptions.

Responsibilities

Keep enough information to understand and reproduce the result.

Inputs and source information

Weather, vegetation, soil hydraulic properties, rooting depth, precipitation, irrigation information, and model settings can affect later modeled states. Keep the source and version information with the results.

Research users and collaborators

Keep the project context, data sources, software version, model settings, and comparison evidence. Report both agreement and disagreement during independent evaluation.

Irrigation interpretation

Modeled irrigation is not the same as documented farmer-applied irrigation unless independent records show that correspondence. A modeled irrigation event is part of the water-balance calculation.

When the model disagrees

A difference from an observation may come from forcing data, parameters, model structure, spatial scale, timing, or measurement uncertainty. Investigate those possibilities before treating the difference as a software failure.

Using study results

Keep evidence tied to the conditions tested.

Performance for one study, crop, climate, period, or configuration should not automatically be extended to every application. Keep each result connected to the supporting study, comparison data, model version, and assumptions.

Important distinction: software usability, engineering reliability, and scientific accuracy are different questions. A functioning workflow still requires scientific interpretation.
Why continuous modeling matters

Important field processes continue between satellite observations.

Irrigation, rainfall, transpiration, soil evaporation, drainage, and root-zone depletion continue between observation times. BAITSSS keeps an hourly accounting of those processes while using observations and meteorological forcing to inform the simulation.
