Access & Participation

Bring the question. We can figure out the right BAITSSS pathway together.

Students, advisors, researchers, universities, agencies, professionals, and organizations are welcome to start a conversation whether the work is an early idea, an active study, or a funded project. You do not need to know BAITSSS in advance or decide first whether you need software, analysis, training, or collaboration.

Open starting point

The first step is simply to describe the problem, study area, period, available evidence, and what you are trying to learn or decide. We can then determine whether BAITSSS is appropriate and what level of involvement makes sense.

01 · Participation pathways

Different projects can enter at different levels.

The goal is not to force every visitor into software access. Some projects may need only guidance, some need a defined analysis, some become research collaborations, and some organizations may want the software or a larger technical integration.

01

Students & advisors

Start with the research question rather than a request for the model itself. We can discuss whether BAITSSS fits the scientific problem and whether the useful next step is guidance, a defined analysis, training, or a broader research collaboration.

02

Academic research / teaching

Universities, laboratories, faculty, and students may use BAITSSS Desktop for research, graduate projects, teaching, and scientific investigation through an appropriate project or institutional arrangement.

03

Government / agency

Public agencies and research organizations may work with BAITSSS on agricultural water, evapotranspiration, irrigation, environmental analysis, field investigation, and related program or research needs.

04

Commercial / professional

Consultants and professional organizations can bring a client or project problem first. Scope, analysis, software use, responsibilities, and technical support can then be defined around the actual need.

05

Scientific collaboration

Researchers and organizations with an existing dataset, study site, proposal, or funded project may collaborate on BAITSSS application, evaluation, scientific development, project-specific analysis, training, and related research.

06

Early discussion / demonstration

If the project is not yet fully defined, that is still a valid starting point. Review the public science and software, request a demonstration, or contact us with the problem so we can determine whether a more substantial pathway is justified.

02 · Documented collaborations

The BAITSSS model has developed through collaboration across universities, agencies, water managers, and student research.

These examples are part of the documented scientific record. They show that collaboration can range from student research to multi-institutional programs and applied water-management studies.

USDA-NIFA · UC RIVERSIDE

AIM-AI multi-institutional research

The BAITSSS model has been used within the USDA-NIFA funded Artificial Intelligence for Sustainable Water, Nutrient, Salinity, and Pest Management in the Western U.S. project led by UC Riverside, including specialty-crop water and irrigation research and collaboration across participating institutions.

KANSAS GMD4 · KANSAS STATE · USDA-ARS

Groundwater and irrigation management

The BAITSSS model was applied with Kansas Groundwater Management District No. 4, Kansas State University, and USDA-ARS collaborators to examine landscape-scale evapotranspiration, modeled irrigation, reported irrigation, and restricted water allocations in northwest Kansas.

KANSAS STATE · USDA-ARS

Field evaluation and crop-water research

Collaborative work at Bushland, Texas brought Kansas State University and USDA-ARS scientists together to evaluate the BAITSSS model against field observations and to investigate crop water use, energy balance behavior, and irrigation-management applications.

USDA-ARS evaluation record ↗

USDA-ARS · UC RIVERSIDE

Specialty-crop evapotranspiration research

Later BAITSSS model research in California included USDA-ARS and UC Riverside collaboration on citrus evapotranspiration, independent eddy-covariance comparison, OpenET comparison, and specialty-crop water-use questions.

USDA-ARS citrus publication record ↗

MIT STUDENT RESEARCH

Higher-resolution data for irrigation management

A documented MIT student collaboration used the BAITSSS model within a university research project in Cheyenne County, Kansas to investigate higher-resolution satellite information for evapotranspiration and irrigation analysis.

03 · Start a conversation

If you have a problem that may fit BAITSSS, the door is open.

You do not need a finished proposal, a software-access request, or a predetermined collaboration structure. Students, advisors, researchers, agencies, consultants, and organizations can begin by describing the problem. If BAITSSS is not the right tool, we should be able to say that early. If it is a reasonable fit, the next step can be sized to the project.
