Access & Participation

Access BAITSSS

Public information, publications, evidence, and demonstrations can be reviewed without software access. Use of BAITSSS Desktop is provided through an approved agreement with the user's institution or organization.

Current status

BAITSSS Desktop is under active development and is not a general public trial. An individual request by itself does not establish software access.

01 · Access pathways

Ways to use and collaborate with BAITSSS

01

Academic research / teaching

Universities, laboratories, faculty, and students may use BAITSSS for research, graduate projects, teaching, and scientific investigation through an appropriate institutional arrangement.

02

Scientific validation

BAITSSS welcomes independent evaluation using field observations, flux measurements, irrigation records, remote-sensing products, or comparison with other models and ET systems.

03

Government / agency

Public agencies and research organizations may collaborate with BAITSSS on agricultural-water, evapotranspiration, irrigation, environmental, or programmatic applications through a defined project or organizational arrangement.

04

Commercial / professional

Professional and commercial applications may be considered where BAITSSS is scientifically appropriate. Scope, access, responsibilities, and support are defined for the intended use.

05

Scientific collaboration

Researchers and organizations with an existing project, dataset, study site, or proposal may collaborate with BAITSSS on model application, evaluation, comparative studies, scientific development, and related research. Project-supported development or training can also be discussed where appropriate.

06

Demonstration / review

Researchers, institutions, reviewers, and prospective collaborators can first examine BAITSSS through the public website, scientific evidence, publications, documentation, videos, and demonstrations before discussing software access or collaboration.

02 · Collaboration in practice

BAITSSS has already been used through funded, multi-institution research

The collaboration pathway is not hypothetical. Earlier and current BAITSSS research shows how a university or agency can bring a funded scientific problem, field data, study sites, or a larger research program and use BAITSSS as part of the work.

Kansas State University + USDA-ARS

Ogallala Aquifer and crop-water research

BAITSSS was developed and evaluated in collaborative research involving Kansas State University and USDA Agricultural Research Service scientists. Work included lysimeter-based evaluation in Bushland, Texas, landscape ET and irrigation analysis in western Kansas, and application at 30 m spatial and hourly temporal resolution. The research was supported by USDA-ARS National Program 211 and in part by the Ogallala Aquifer Program, a consortium that included USDA-ARS and Kansas State University. The collaboration produced peer-reviewed and USDA-documented BAITSSS research rather than a software demonstration alone.

USDA-NIFA multi-institution project

BAITSSS inside a broader funded research program

BAITSSS has also been used within a USDA-NIFA-funded western U.S. research project led by the University of California, Riverside. Project reporting describes BAITSSS use for specialty crops, field validation, high-resolution satellite and meteorological inputs, soil-property integration, irrigation assessment, and comparison with independent ET observations and OpenET. This illustrates another collaboration model: the research program supplies the larger scientific objective and resources, while BAITSSS provides a modeling framework that can be applied, evaluated, and extended within the project.

MIT student collaboration

Higher-resolution data for irrigation management

An MIT student collaboration used BAITSSS to investigate how higher-resolution PlanetScope satellite observations could improve evapotranspiration and irrigation analysis in Cheyenne County, Kansas. The work examined whether finer spatial information could better resolve field and within-field variability and support more detailed irrigation-management decisions, with BAITSSS providing the modeling framework and OpenET used as an independent comparison.

03 · Independent research evidence

BAITSSS described and used beyond its originating publications

Independent and institutional records are especially important because they show how BAITSSS has been used, interpreted, or reported outside the model author's own publication record.

USDA multistate research reporting

Groundwater management and LEMA decision support

A USDA multistate accomplishments record describes BAITSSS application in the Northwest Kansas Local Enhanced Management Area and reports that model results identified opportunities to reduce irrigation and pumping costs without reducing crop yield. The same institutional record states that BAITSSS provided guidance to the LEMA in adopting data-driven policies.

USDA multistate research · 2025

Soil information and irrigation requirements

A 2025 USDA multistate annual report documents BAITSSS use in Yuma to examine how alternative soil-property datasets affect modeled irrigation requirements and evapotranspiration. The work compared SSURGO and POLARIS and showed that differences in available water capacity can materially change modeled irrigation frequency and water use.

USDA multistate research · 2024

Specialty-crop ET and field observations

A 2024 USDA multistate annual report describes BAITSSS in California citrus research alongside eddy-covariance measurements and OpenET. The reporting places BAITSSS within a broader effort to improve evapotranspiration assessment by integrating field observations with remote-sensing and modeling approaches.

04 · Project collaboration

Planning a project involving BAITSSS?

Contact us early when project-specific analysis, validation, development, training, or other substantial participation may need to be included in the project scope.
