Business

What capability already exists in BAITSSS?

That is the starting point for evaluating BAITSSS as a technology asset. The case is not based on the amount of source code or on the fact that a desktop application exists. It is based on the scientific, computational, spatial, operational, and product capabilities that already work together in BAITSSS Desktop V1.

A company considering BAITSSS can compare those capabilities with what it already has, what it plans to build, and what it would rather obtain through integration, licensing, partnership, or acquisition.
Capability inventory

BAITSSS as it exists today.

The items below are drawn from the maintained BAITSSS capability inventory. They are grouped by what the system can do rather than by individual screens or code modules. Items still under development are not counted as current capability.

Scientific and field-state capability
01

Hourly two-source energy balance

Represents soil and vegetation as separate components within the energy-balance calculation.

02

Separated ET components

Produces vegetation ET and soil ET separately, together with total evapotranspiration.

03

Two-layer soil-water balance

Maintains distinct surface-layer and root-zone soil-water states.

04

Hour-to-hour state carryover

Current soil-water and related model states depend on the preceding simulation state rather than being calculated as isolated snapshots.

05

Coupled water, vegetation, irrigation, and thermal behavior

Vegetation, energy balance, soil water, irrigation, and modeled thermal behavior operate within the same simulation trajectory.

06

Modeled irrigation requirement

Represents irrigation requirement within the maintained scientific route and supports management controls such as allowed depletion and rooting-depth-related configuration where exposed.

07

Scenario reruns

The same field can be rerun with supported management or scientific configuration changes for comparison.

08

Modeled canopy and soil temperature

Produces internal canopy and soil thermal behavior for scientific inspection where supported by run outputs.

09

Independent Landsat thermal comparison

Observed Landsat surface temperature can be extracted at the exact acquisition time for comparison without being assimilated into the modeled trajectory.

Satellite, weather, and spatial capability
10

Landsat vegetation preparation

Supports Landsat 7, 8, and 9 scene handling, AOI-aware processing, NDVI derivation, LAI representation, and seasonal vegetation progression.

11

Continuous hourly weather forcing

Uses NLDAS hourly meteorology with complete-period coverage checks and internal reference-ET preparation.

12

30 m field-scale model grid

Runs spatial projects on a Landsat-based 30 m model grid with CRS, extent, compatibility, and alignment checks before scientific execution.

13

Whole-AOI spatial simulation

Executes the scientific model across the defined field or Area of Interest rather than limiting use to a single point.

14

Selected-pixel scientific history

A model-grid pixel can be selected and its historical hourly trajectory exported from spatial or supported single-cell runs.

15

Environmental base-data preparation

Uses elevation, land cover, Field Capacity, and Available Water Capacity with project-grid preparation and validation before execution.

Execution and scientific-control capability
16

Controlled scientific execution path

The maintained ET, soil-water, irrigation, and energy-balance calculations follow a defined scientific route.

17

Prepared-data reuse

Recurring weather, vegetation, raster, and model inputs are prepared for reuse rather than repeatedly reconstructed inside the simulation path.

18

Multiprocessing spatial execution

Spatial workload can be distributed across processor resources while the time-dependent sequence required by the model is preserved.

19

Committed-hour checkpointing

A simulation hour is committed only after the required state and outputs are in place for safe continuation.

20

Stop, pause, resume, and recovery

Long runs can stop at committed boundaries and continue from the next uncommitted hour when a valid checkpoint exists.

21

Parameter authority

Scientific settings, fixed coefficients, derived values, and runtime values have defined ownership so hidden fallbacks do not silently redefine a run.

22

Frozen run configuration

Scientific configuration is fixed at run start so later project edits do not silently change an active or completed run.

23

Run provenance and traceability

Run identity, selected data sources, configuration, checkpoints, outputs, and export metadata remain associated for review and reproduction.

24

Bounded long-run storage

Older hourly spatial outputs can be compacted losslessly while historical values remain available to Results and finalization.

Product and transfer capability
25

Persistent project and run management

Projects, independent runs, simulation periods, run status, checkpoints, validation records, and Results remain separately identified.

26

Input-readiness gates

Required spatial, weather, and project data are checked before scientific execution rather than leaving major compatibility failures to the running model.

27

Run-specific Results

Mapped ET, spatial variability, time series, soil-water states, irrigation, vegetation, weather context, and selected-pixel analysis stay tied to the selected run.

28

Scientific export and reporting

Supports scientific CSV exports, selected-pixel exports, metadata, PNG output, figure modes, and report output where exposed by the Results workflow.

29

Packaged Windows runtime

The BAITSSS application and its scientific runtime are distributed together for the maintained desktop route rather than requiring the user to assemble a Python and GIS environment.

30

Separation of application payload and user data

Projects and completed run outputs are kept separately from the replaceable application payload so software maintenance does not depend on discarding scientific work.

The asset is the combination.

None of these items should be treated as valuable merely because it has a technical name. The business question is what an organization would have to reproduce to obtain the same scientific behavior, spatial workflow, long-run execution, reproducibility controls, and usable product foundation, and whether BAITSSS can shorten that work.

First market reading

What the comparison tells us to investigate next.

Several capabilities are clearly common in the market: satellite monitoring, weather, soil-moisture information, irrigation recommendations, dashboards, sensors, APIs, and field-data integration. BAITSSS should not present those alone as a reason to be acquired. The more useful questions are where the combination or depth of the BAITSSS scientific system is less commonly visible.

Candidate distinction

Partitioned ET inside a coupled model

BAITSSS separately represents soil and vegetation ET within an hourly two-source energy balance. Many commercial products publish total ET, crop ET, water stress, or water balance. Whether they expose the same soil and vegetation partitioning as an operating product capability needs a direct technical comparison.

Candidate distinction

Two soil-water states carried hourly

BAITSSS maintains surface-layer and root-zone soil-water states through time. Commercial platforms commonly report soil moisture or water balance, but public product pages often do not state how those states are physically represented or propagated.

Candidate distinction

One seasonal scientific trajectory

BAITSSS couples vegetation, energy balance, soil water, irrigation, and thermal behavior from hour to hour. That continuous state trajectory may be more important than any one output. We should test how often comparable commercial systems make this type of coupled simulation explicit.

Engineering depth

Scientific restart, not only job restart

Checkpointing in BAITSSS preserves committed scientific state and resumes from the next uncommitted hour. That matters because the model is stateful. We should compare this with the resilience claims made by scientific and enterprise modeling platforms, not ordinary desktop applications.

Engineering depth

Authority and reproducibility are part of the product

Frozen run configuration, data-source state, parameter authority, run-specific Results, checkpoints, and export metadata are maintained together. This is especially relevant for research, regulated, institutional, and technical due-diligence use.

Market gap to examine

Physics-based field state beneath larger platforms

Several established businesses are very strong in sensors, control, imagery, machinery, cloud delivery, customer workflow, and enterprise scale. A possible role for BAITSSS is narrower: provide a developed ET and soil-water simulation layer where a larger platform wants deeper physical field-state modeling.

No uniqueness claim is made here yet.

The next test is technical and company-specific. For each BAITSSS capability, we need to determine whether comparable platforms provide it directly, approximate it through another method, keep it internal, or do not make it publicly evident. Only capabilities that survive that review should be emphasized as a BAITSSS point of distinction.

Business use

Where this capability could matter to another organization.

BAITSSS does not have to replace an established platform. A larger organization may already have customers, sensors, equipment, imagery, cloud infrastructure, APIs, sales, or field operations. The question is whether BAITSSS adds a useful scientific layer to that existing system.

Irrigation

Scientific layer beneath scheduling and control

Use ET, root-zone and surface soil-water state, modeled irrigation requirement, and field-scale simulation beneath an existing controller, telemetry, or scheduling system.

Digital agriculture

Water-state capability inside a broader agronomy platform

Connect BAITSSS outputs and state trajectories to a system that already handles growers, fields, crop records, machinery, recommendations, and enterprise workflow.

Remote sensing

Move from imagery to time-evolving physical state

Use satellite vegetation and spatial context as inputs to a model that carries field water and energy states between observations.

Sensors and IoT

Pair measurement with model state

Use field observations to complement or eventually condition a spatial and temporal scientific model rather than relying only on individual measurement locations.

Water management

Field-scale scientific outputs for larger systems

Provide ET, soil-water, irrigation, and water-balance information to institutional analysis, reporting, planning, or water-management workflows.

Research and product development

Start new work above an existing foundation

Use the maintained scientific and execution system as a baseline for new research, proprietary data connections, product experiments, or deployment models instead of rebuilding the full stack first.
